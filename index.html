Books

A collection of books I've read, along with my personal thoughts and takeaways.

The Most Important Thing

Howard Marks

Click to expand

This is probably my favorite book and what set me on my path to study business. Howard Marks uses his experiences at Citi and Oaktree to explain the complex world of our capital markets in a way that can stick with the average reader.

Purchase Book

Everything Is Predictable

Tom Chivers

Click to expand

As an accounting and finance major, this book does a good job of explaining Bayesian statistics and why it matters. After reading this, hopefully you will understand the difference between Bayesian and Frequentist statistics and why our digital world is favoring the former more and more.

Purchase Book

Irrational Exuberance

Robert J. Shiller

Click to expand

As I read more and more books on economics, I found that Irrational Exuberance was one of the most frequently quoted pieces in financial literature. In the source document, Robert Shiller challenges the efficient market hypothesis and uses the Shiller P/E ratio to illustrate that asset prices do not always reflect business reality.

Purchase Book

Leadership Is An Art

Max De Pree

Click to expand

This book was given to me by an executive at AdventHealth during a one-on-one meeting back in highschool. The book is very well written and advocates for the idea of servant leadership. It also gives background on Herman Miller, the maker of the famous Eames chair.

Purchase Book

Nuclear Fusion

Sharon Ann Holgate

Click to expand

After watching Oppenheimer, by Christopher Nolan. I became intersted in all things nuclear. This book gives good background on how nuclear technologies have developed through the years, the two leading methods for achieving fusion (tokomaks and stellarators), and how the complexity of the technology frequently pushes projects far off schedule and way over budget.

Purchase Book

1929

Andrew Ross Sorkin

Click to expand

This is a popular one so I won't say much about it. 1929 was a great read for those interested in American history or how the individuals at the top of our financial institutions have the power to change our economic fates.

Purchase Book

Venture Deals

Brad Feld

Click to expand

Venture Deals is a MUST READ for anyone intersted in starting their own company, raising venture capital, or investing as a VC. The book is slightly technical, but that's what makes it so good. You learn about the items on a term-sheet, what parties are involved in a fundraise, and how every subsequent round effects each shareholder.

Purchase Book

The Grid

Gretchen Bakke

Click to expand

This book pushes for increased investment in the U.S.'s grid given the average age of major generation plants and transmission lines. That said, this book was writted before the A.I. boom and ongoing investments in electric infrastructure. I'd like to read an updated version of this book when available.

Purchase Book
